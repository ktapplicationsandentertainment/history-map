What you can do with it

Move the year control anywhere from roughly 123,000 BCE to 2019 CE and watch the whole world's political map redraw itself — empires expanding and collapsing, colonial borders shifting, new nations appearing after independence and decolonization. Click any region to see its name, which power it was subject to, roughly how long it existed, and — for hundreds of well-documented countries — real recorded history: predecessor and successor states, and day-precise boundary-change dates sourced from community-mapped historical records.

Want to show it to a class or share a specific moment? Every view has its own link — jump to 1815 after the Congress of Vienna, 1945 after WWII, or 1960 in the middle of African decolonization, and the URL captures exactly that year. There's also a built-in tool to record a play-through across any range of years as a downloadable video, for dropping straight into a lesson or a presentation.

Built for the classroom, not just for fun

This started as a personal passion project and grew into something meant to hold up as an actual teaching resource: no account walls, no ads, no dark patterns — just a map. Every data source is documented, every known limitation is written down rather than hidden, and the boundary data itself comes from a combination of academic and community-maintained historical GIS datasets, cross-checked against each other. Read the full methodology and data sources before citing anything here in serious research — it's a genuinely useful map, not a peer-reviewed atlas.
