How this map is built

This page explains where the data comes from, how it's combined, and — just as important — where it's known to be wrong, approximate, or incomplete. Read this before citing anything here in a lesson plan or paper.

What this is

A personal passion project: a whole-world map of political boundaries for any year in history, with a year control to move through time. It's also meant to double as a free research/teaching resource for history classrooms — but it is not a peer-reviewed academic source. Verify anything load-bearing against a primary source.

The boundaries are drawn over today's coastlines and rivers, not the physical geography of the selected year — physical geography (coastlines, river courses, lake extents) does change over centuries, and this map doesn't attempt to model that. See "Known limitations" below.

Data sources

historical-basemaps (Ourednik et al.) ~123,000 BCE – 1880 CE (43 snapshot years) GPLv3 The base boundary geometry for ancient through early-modern history. Community-maintained, explicitly labeled work-in-progress by its own authors.
CShapes 2.0 (Schvitz, Rüegger, Girardin, Cederman, Weidmann, Gleditsch — ETH Zürich) 1886 – 2019 (134 snapshot years, one per calendar year) CC BY-NC-SA 4.0 Academic dataset, replaces historical-basemaps' sparser coverage of the same era. Adds capital city per country.
Wikidata Modern countries only (matched by name, ~174 of 181) CC0 Replaces the derived existed-date-range with real recorded inception/dissolution dates, plus predecessor/successor links, wherever a confident name match exists.
OpenHistoricalMap 233 entities, wherever community contributors have mapped them (very uneven — see below) CC0 Replaces the boundary shape itself (not just the date) with a more precise, real, day-dated version, for whichever specific entity/year combinations it covers. How the sources are combined

Every year is one file with the whole world's boundaries for that year. The three map sources never overlap in which years they own — historical-basemaps covers up to 1880, CShapes takes over for 1886–2019 (its snapshots replace historical-basemaps' overlapping years outright, rather than showing both). OpenHistoricalMap is different: it doesn't own any years by itself. Instead, wherever it has a matching, date-accurate boundary for a specific country in a specific year, that one entity's shape is swapped in on top of whatever the base source already drew — everything else on the map for that year is untouched. That's why some countries get a gold outline and most don't: it reflects genuinely uneven real-world coverage, not a rendering choice.

Simplification: all geometry is simplified (fewer vertices) before shipping to the browser, to keep the map fast to load. One deliberate choice here: the pipeline does not run typical GIS "cleanup" that dissolves overlapping polygons or removes small/sliver shapes — overlapping claims and small polities are real, intentional features of pre-modern history in this data, not defects to clean up.

Known limitations

  • Physical geography is modern, political geography is historical. The basemap shows today's coastlines/rivers. The Aral Sea, for instance, looked very different a few decades ago — the map won't show that.
  • "Boundaries" are a modern concept. Before the Peace of Westphalia (1648), the idea of a fixed, linear national border doesn't really describe how pre-modern polities worked. Treat early borders as approximate zones of influence, not surveyed lines.
  • Overlapping regions are intentional in ancient/pre-modern periods (competing or overlapping spheres of authority) — not a rendering bug.
  • Existed-date ranges are usually derived, not verified. For any entity without a confident Wikidata match, the popup's date range is just the min/max year that exact name string happens to appear across this dataset's snapshots. Renames, splits, and mergers make this wrong in plenty of cases — a visible caveat appears when a name's appearances have a gap in the middle, which is the clearest sign the naive range is suspect.
  • OpenHistoricalMap coverage is very uneven and reflects individual contributors' interests, not a systematic effort. Some entities (e.g. France) have decades of richly detailed, sourced boundary history. Most have none at all. A country having no gold outline says nothing about that country's actual history — only that nobody has mapped it there yet.
  • Entity matching across sources is name-based and imperfect. Different sources call the same country different things (historical-lineage names vs. common names, local-script names vs. English), so a small number of matches are hand-corrected rather than fully automatic. Mismatches are possible and not always caught.
  • A few entities are known to be missing outright due to broken source geometry rather than any historical reality — most notably Switzerland in the 1994/2000/2010 historical-basemaps-era snapshots (fixed everywhere CShapes/OHM cover instead).

Source code

The full data pipeline and front end are open — every step described above is a script, not a manual process, so it can be re-run, audited, or corrected. See the GitHub repository.
